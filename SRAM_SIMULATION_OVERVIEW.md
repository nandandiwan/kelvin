# Kelvin: overview of SRAM thermal simulation

Status checked: **2026-09-16**, against the current working tree, including the
periodic boundaries, verified instance-to-channel power mapping, integrated
notebook-derived SRAM mesh pipeline, and layout-linked interconnect Joule heat.

## 1. What Kelvin is doing

Kelvin estimates **where heat is generated and how temperature spreads through
an SRAM structure**. It combines a layout-derived geometry, prescribed or
compact-model-derived electrical power, and a finite-element heat-conduction
solver.

For the main compact-model case, it models **one SRAM bitcell**, not the entire
memory macro. The calculation is one-way: SPICE supplies transistor and local
interconnect/contact powers to
the thermal solver; the resulting temperatures are **not fed back into SPICE**.
It is not a transistor transport/TCAD solver or a fully coupled electrothermal
signoff flow.

There are two geometry paths:

- **Default for the compact and general transient bitcell cases:** original
  polygon footprints, assembled into a complete substrate/dielectric/conductor
  mesh using the same backend as `read_gds.ipynb`.
- **Explicit legacy comparison, and the other SRAM cases unless noted below:**
  a simplified, bounding-box GDS geometry built by `mesh/gds_volume.py`.

The earlier layer-by-layer SRAM comparison files remain previews. They are not
the complete mesh now generated by the integrated pipeline.

## 2. Main bitcell workflow

The entry point is [cases/run_bitcell_compact.py](cases/run_bitcell_compact.py).

```text
GDS layout                       Extracted bitcell circuit + SKY130 models
        |                                         |
Select sram_sp_cell                         ngspice READ/WRITE transient
        |                                         |
Find device/channel footprints             Local channel heat + resistor I²R
        |                                         |
Assign z-stack and materials               Integrate event / average by access rate
        |                                         |
        +------ Map channels and local wire/contact heat ------+
                                   |
                   Gmsh: tagged 3D tetrahedral mesh
                                   |
              DOLFINx/PETSc: solve heat conduction with BCs
                                   |
             Temperature field, hotspot, plots, power balance
```

### Geometry and scope

The layout is [data/sram22_64x22m4w22.gds](data/sram22_64x22m4w22.gds), but the
case extracts only `sram_sp_cell`: **1.20 × 1.58 µm**, with area **1.896 µm²**.
The corresponding [bitcell netlist](data/sram_sp_cell.spice) has six functional
SRAM transistors and two additional extracted devices with drain and source
connected to the same node. Those two receive zero `|Id × Vds|` power.

GDS provides **lateral masks**, not heights, thermal properties, package
cooling, or operating activity. Kelvin supplies those additional assumptions.
Pin, implant and annotation purposes are not automatically separate material
films, even when they appear as separate GDS layers.

The default geometry call chain is:

```text
cases/run_bitcell_compact.py / cases/run_bitcell_transient.py
  → mesh/sram.py
  → mesh/gds_notebook.py (shared with read_gds.ipynb)
  → tagged MSH + material-regions JSON manifest
  → mesh/gds_import.py
  → thermal solver
```

The shared backend preserves mask polygons, assigns their heights and materials,
fills the surrounding substrate/dielectric, and constructs a conformal thermal
mesh. The SRAM adapter matches each source polygon to its verified SPICE
instance, then assigns that instance's power. Coordinates are converted from
micrometres to metres on import.

With `--mesh-method legacy`, the two drivers instead call
`mesh/gds_build.py` and `mesh/gds_volume.py`. In that path,
[`_window_rects()`](mesh/gds_volume.py) replaces clipped polygons with bounding
rectangles and extrudes them into boxes within background-material slabs.

`--pad-um` adds background around the bitcell. It does **not** add actual
neighboring transistors or wiring.

### Vertical stack and materials

The SRAM notebook adapter sets explicit stack assumptions in
[mesh/sram.py](mesh/sram.py), aligned with the principal nominal dimensions of
the legacy frontside stack in [gds/techmap.py](gds/techmap.py):

| Region | Thickness used by the SRAM adapter |
| --- | ---: |
| Silicon below diffusion | 50.2062 µm |
| Active/diffusion | 120 nm total |
| Channel heat-source sublayer | Top 10 nm of that active layer, not an extra 10 nm |
| Polysilicon | 180 nm |
| Local interconnect, LI1 | 100 nm |
| MCON | 340 nm |
| Metal 1 / Metal 2 | 360 nm each |
| Via between M1 and M2 | 270 nm |
| Passivation | 1 µm nominal |

The new adapter places the active top at `z=0`, the backside at `−50.3262 µm`,
and the passivation top at `3.04 µm`: the total thickness is 53.3662 µm. It uses
SiO₂ outside the active polygons and 1 µm of SiN passivation. The channel source
occupies the upper 10 nm of active, with the remaining 110 nm represented below
it. LICON runs from its assigned poly or diffusion landing height to LI1.

Much of the nominal stack follows Magic
extraction-height data, but **these are modeling inputs, not a verified physical
cross-section of this SRAM**. Substrate truncation, well representation,
channel heat-deposition depth, nominal passivation and omission of gate oxide
are additional approximations.

[spec/materials.py](spec/materials.py) supplies thermal properties. For example,
the model uses conductivities of 148 W/(m·K) for bulk silicon, 70 for doped
source/drain silicon, 20 for channel silicon, 1.4 for SiO₂, 30 for LI1/TiN and
polysilicon, 170 for tungsten contacts, and 220 for aluminum interconnect.
These are engineering assumptions, not values extracted from the GDS.

## 3. How heat generation is assigned

### Main compact-model bitcell case

[gds/spice_netlist.py](gds/spice_netlist.py) builds ngspice decks using the
bundled SKY130 BSIM3 models and a 1.8 V supply.
[gds/spice_transient.py](gds/spice_transient.py) saves validated electrical
READ/WRITE traces. Its BSIM3 intrinsic channel current excludes displacement
current; heat is computed per physical transistor as:

```text
P_device(t) = Id_channel(t) × |Vd(t) - Vs(t)|
```

The default is a transient-SPICE **READ-1**, with explicitly precharged
bitlines and a stored state that is verified to survive the access. Both
READ states and both WRITE transitions are supported. The shared
[thermal workload adapter](gds/thermal_workload.py) integrates the complete
event, including settling. Both the steady solver and averaged heating mode use:

```text
E_device = integral(P_device(t) dt)
P_average = E_device × row_hit_rate / period
```

The pulsed thermal solver uses each trace's integrated heat over the actual
thermal step, not a rectangular proxy or instantaneous sample. All eight
consumed source-energy integrals are independently checked against SPICE.
The compact driver defaults to `row_hit_rate=1`, but exposes it and the period
as controls. The 0.239519 ns WL plateau and 3.86681 ns repetition period are
borrowed from the **different-sized** `sram22_2048x8m8w1` Liberty file; electrical
temperature is fixed at 25 °C. These remain testbench assumptions.

The former forced crowbar snapshot and DC-based READ/HOLD/settled-WRITE are
available only through explicit `--power-model dc-surrogate`. They are not
silently substituted for real READ/WRITE waveforms. See
[SPICE_TRANSIENT.md](SPICE_TRANSIENT.md) for commands and remaining limitations.

Channels are identified from `diffusion ∩ polysilicon`. A **cell-specific,
netlist-assisted connectivity map** now associates all eight SPICE instances
with their individual original-GDS channel footprints. Every instance retains
its own power, including asymmetric READ/HOLD/WRITE states. Mirrored array
placement carries instance identity with the transformed polygon.

The former representative-per-class duplication was removed on 2026-09-16.
The map rejects changed source GDS, relevant masks or bundled netlists, and a
per-instance mesh audit compares integrated heat against the original electrical
power dictionary. It is not general-purpose LVS: logical WL stitching and the
two short parasitic devices' D=S interpretation remain explicit contracts of
the bundled extracted netlist. See [SRAM_INSTANCE_MAPPING.md](SRAM_INSTANCE_MAPPING.md).

Each assigned device power becomes uniform volumetric heat in its source region:

```text
q = P / source_volume       [W/m³]
```

This does not resolve the actual nonuniform dissipation along a transistor
channel or near its drain.

### Interconnect and contact heating

The notebook-mesh READ/WRITE drivers now default to `--interconnect layout`.
An approximate clipped-sheet resistance network connects the real poly, LI1,
metal and contact geometry to the compact transistor terminals. Each resistor's
`(Va-Vb)²/R` waveform comes from the same SPICE run as the transistor power;
the transistor powers are recomputed using their actual local terminal voltages.

Resistor polygon-prism supports are intersected with the existing thermal
tetrahedra and projected conservatively. Powers are not spread over entire
layer tags. Actual consumed energy is audited spatially and by channel/layer.
The old channel-only flow is available with `--interconnect none`.

This is **not full foundry RC extraction**: the existing estimated 16 fF
bitline load is retained, with no newly extracted wire capacitances. External
driver and shared-array routing losses are excluded from the bitcell heat.
See [INTERCONNECT_HEATING.md](INTERCONNECT_HEATING.md) for parameters,
electrical boundary assumptions, commands and verification.

Other paths use different power models. The generic GDS source builder splits
a supplied total into **70% channel / 30% contact** using geometric heuristics;
the compact case bypasses that split. Whole-macro studies may obtain the total
from Liberty leakage and access/idle energies. The READ helper uses an assumed
16 fF bitline capacitance and 0.1 V sense margin to estimate access energy.
Those older heuristic paths are not the new layout-linked interconnect model.

## 4. Heat equation, numerical method and boundaries

The steady and transient equations are:

```text
Steady:       −∇·(k ∇T) = q
Transient:    ρ cp ∂T/∂t − ∇·(k ∇T) = q(t)
```

Here `T` is temperature, `k` conductivity, `ρ cp` volumetric heat capacity,
and `q` generated heat per unit volume.

[physics/coeffs.py](physics/coeffs.py) assigns piecewise-constant properties
and heat sources to mesh elements. Temperature uses continuous first-order
finite elements. [solve/steady.py](solve/steady.py) solves the steady system;
[solve/transient.py](solve/transient.py) uses implicit backward Euler for time
integration. PETSc uses conjugate gradients with hypre BoomerAMG and a relative
linear-solver tolerance of `1e-10`; the current solvers raise errors on failed
convergence.

The SRAM paths use constant scalar material properties at their 300 K reference
values. The available `k_at(T)` helper is not used to update conductivity during
these solves. Interfaces have perfect thermal contact; there is no explicit
interface thermal resistance or separate electron/phonon temperature model.

### Default boundaries for the compact and general bitcell-transient cases

| Boundary | Applied condition | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Backside | Robin: `−k ∇T·n = h(T − T_ambient)` | Finite heat loss through an assumed package resistance |
| Top | Zero normal heat flux | No heat escapes through the top |
| Opposite x/y sides | Periodic temperature constraints | The modeled geometry and activity repeat by translation |

The backside uses **h = 20,000 W/(m²·K)** and **T_ambient = 300 K**. It is not
a fixed-temperature backside. Its coefficient is an assumed effective cooling
parameter, not derived from this GDS or calibrated for this particular package.

With this coefficient applied over one 1.896 µm² footprint, the effective
backside resistance is about **26.4 K per µW**. At steady state, with no other
net heat sink:

```text
mean(T_backside) − T_ambient = P_total / (h × footprint_area)
```

That explains why the overall rise can remain similar after replacing insulating
sides with periodic sides: periodicity adds no net lateral cooling. It can
still change local temperature gradients and heat paths.

Periodic boundaries allow heat to cross paired faces. They are **not insulating
walls or reflection-symmetry planes**. The constraint is implemented in the
finite-element system through `dolfinx_mpc`, not by editing temperatures after
the solve. One-cell periodicity assumes identical orientation and activity in
every copy; the actual macro's mirrored cells or nonuniform activity can require
a larger repeat unit. See [PERIODIC_BOUNDARIES.md](PERIODIC_BOUNDARIES.md).

## 5. Other SRAM studies in the repository

Not every SRAM script uses the same workload or lateral boundaries.

| Entry point | What it studies | Lateral boundaries |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [run_bitcell_compact.py](cases/run_bitcell_compact.py) | Steady average of transient-SPICE event heat; READ-1 by default, both WRITE directions supported | Periodic by default; optional insulating |
| [run_bitcell_transient.py](cases/run_bitcell_transient.py) | Same event-average heating or actual SPICE waveforms with `--instantaneous`, followed by zero-source cooldown | Periodic by default; optional insulating |
| [run_bitcell_gallery.py](cases/run_bitcell_gallery.py) | Multiple DC operating points and row-hit rates | Insulating |
| [run_bitcell_access_transient.py](cases/run_bitcell_access_transient.py) | Raw crowbar power held on over a short window; defaults to 4 ns with 50 ps steps | Insulating; optional lateral Robin |
| [run_sram_array_transient.py](cases/run_sram_array_transient.py), [run_array_full.py](cases/run_array_full.py) | Explicitly tiled, mirrored bitcells; default 3 × 5 patch with pulsed power on one row | Approximate lateral Robin, `h=k/r` |
| [run_gds_coarse_solve.py](cases/run_gds_coarse_solve.py), [render_sram_heatmap.py](cases/render_sram_heatmap.py) | Tile-averaged whole-macro temperature | Case-specific; not the compact periodic-cell setup |
| [run_gds_submodel.py](cases/run_gds_submodel.py) | Fine window with lateral temperatures interpolated from a coarse macro result | Prescribed lateral temperatures |

Only `run_bitcell_compact.py` and `run_bitcell_transient.py` currently default
to the notebook-derived mesh. The gallery, access-transient and explicitly tiled
array cases still use the legacy geometry; the coarse/submodel paths retain
their existing separate approximations. Integrating the new bitcell mesh does
not silently migrate those studies.

The tiled patch is constructed from a selected cell; it is not the complete
macro extracted as placed. The coarse model homogenizes materials and power,
so it does not resolve individual transistor hotspots. Submodeling is one-way:
the fine window does not update the coarse solution. The lateral `h=k/r`
condition is an approximate continuation of heat conduction into surrounding
material, **not electromagnetic/thermal surface radiation**.

The two main thermal drivers now use transient-SPICE event heat by default.
`run_bitcell_transient.py --instantaneous` follows the time-resolved waveform;
steady and sustained-average runs use the same integrated event energy.
Electrical traces are precomputed at fixed temperature, so this remains
one-way coupling rather than electrothermal feedback. Legacy DC rectangles
and averaged surrogates require explicit `--power-model dc-surrogate`.

The electrical transient path has now been repaired: all decks specify
micrometre geometry units, READ has explicit switched precharge, and completed
READ/WRITE traces retain all eight devices' intrinsic channel heat. Both stored
states and both WRITE directions are verified. See
[SPICE_TRANSIENT.md](SPICE_TRANSIENT.md) for commands, timestep convergence and
limits. The default uses a fixed WL pulse, not the legacy 0.1 V READ
sense-margin surrogate, and does not include peripheral heat or idle leakage
outside the electrical observation window.
Old DC-derived powers and temperatures also predate the unit correction and
must be regenerated; old numbers are not validation of the corrected system.

## 6. Old versus new geometry: current status

The old bounding-box approximation can change **material connectivity**, not
just drawing style. The saved LI1 audit for this cell found:

| LI1 representation | Separate LI1 pieces | Footprint area |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Exact GDS union / new prism preview | 6 | 0.8949 µm² |
| Old thermal mesh | 3 | 1.4242 µm² |

The old mesh fills real gaps, including a 140 nm gap, and adds about 59% LI1
area. Two overlapping rail extensions legitimately reduce eight raw GDS
polygons to six pieces; the further reduction to three is an approximation
error. These counts refer to LI1 connectivity, not circuit net counts.
See the [LI1 diagnostic](out/sram_mesh_comparison/li1_connectivity.svg) and
[verification data](out/sram_mesh_comparison/li1_connectivity.json).

The clean reference drawing from [mesh/gds_svg_viz.py](mesh/gds_svg_viz.py)
renders original polygons. **A faithful-looking geometry SVG does not prove
that the old solver mesh has those same footprints.**

The [notebook](../read_gds.ipynb) and SRAM drivers now share
[mesh/gds_notebook.py](mesh/gds_notebook.py), rather than maintaining separate
geometry implementations. [mesh/sram.py](mesh/sram.py) adds the SRAM-specific
stack configuration, exact source-polygon/instance correspondence and saved-mesh
checks. [mesh/gds_import.py](mesh/gds_import.py) imports the tagged MSH and JSON
manifest, validates the thermal domain and assigns the per-instance powers.
The shared builder uses Kelvin's `PolySi` material name for polysilicon.

The former `0.02975 µm² of licon1 does not land on poly/diff` blocker is resolved
by classifying **whole contact cuts by positive-area overlap**, not clipping
their footprints to the lower conductor. Each of the ten distinct SRAM LICON
cuts must overlap exactly one lower category (poly or diffusion/tap) and LI1
above. Partial lower/upper coverage is accepted only when the entire cut lies
inside the explicit memory-core marker `(81,2)`; floating, ambiguous or
edge-touch-only cuts remain errors. The PDK identifies `(81,2)` as `areaid.ce`,
and marks several LICON enclosure rules as periphery-only, outside that marker.
See the primary [PDK layer definitions](https://skywater-pdk.readthedocs.io/en/main/rules/layers.html)
and [LICON/periphery rules](https://skywater-pdk.readthedocs.io/en/main/rules/periphery.html).

This is a documented thermal-geometry interpretation, **not foundry DRC or a
reconstruction of the fabricated contact profile**. The whole cut is extruded
as a uniform prism to its assigned landing plane, including mask overhangs.
All contact/via levels still undergo per-cut landing validation. See
[SRAM_CONTACT_GEOMETRY.md](SRAM_CONTACT_GEOMETRY.md) for measured overlap areas,
the validation policy and its limitations.

The generic notebook's 2 µm substrate and 120 nm source defaults do not silently
replace the SRAM settings: the adapter explicitly uses 50.3262 µm below active
top, a 10 nm channel source, SiO₂ field background and 1 µm SiN passivation.
A reused notebook manifest retains **its own recorded geometry settings**;
inspect them before comparing runs. Historical
[mesh-comparison notes](out/sram_mesh_comparison/README.md) describe the earlier
preview stage, while [GDS_IMPORT.md](GDS_IMPORT.md) describes the import interface.

## 7. Running the integrated bitcell model

These commands select the **notebook-derived polygon mesh**, now the default
for these two entry points. Run from `kelvin/`. On this machine, the compatible
Python and locally installed dependencies are:

```bash
cd /global/home/krishnabhattaram/ThermalSimulation/kelvin
export KELVIN_PYTHON=/global/home/krishnabhattaram/Neural-Network-Materials/Electrostats/env/bin/python
export PYTHONPATH="$PWD/.deps/dolfinx-mpc-0.10/python${PYTHONPATH:+:$PYTHONPATH}"
export KELVIN_NGSPICE="$PWD/.deps/ngspice/bin/ngspice"
export PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1
export XDG_CACHE_HOME=/tmp/kelvin-sram-cache
export MPLCONFIGDIR=/tmp/kelvin-sram-mpl

# Serial execution on this restricted host only; omit for multi-process runs.
export UCX_TLS=self
export UCX_LOG_LEVEL=error

"$KELVIN_PYTHON" -c 'import dolfinx, dolfinx_mpc, gdstk, gmsh'
"$KELVIN_NGSPICE" --version

# Steady average from a validated transient-SPICE READ event.
"$KELVIN_PYTHON" cases/run_bitcell_compact.py \
    --mesh-method notebook --point read_1 \
    --lateral-bc periodic --out-dir out/sram_overview_steady

# Follow the actual READ waveform, then cool down.
"$KELVIN_PYTHON" cases/run_bitcell_transient.py \
    --mesh-method notebook --point read_1 --instantaneous --lateral-bc periodic \
    --out-dir out/sram_overview_transient

# Reuse the completed mesh for a switching WRITE without remeshing.
"$KELVIN_PYTHON" cases/run_bitcell_compact.py \
    --mesh-method notebook \
    --mesh-manifest out/sram_overview_steady/sram_sp_cell_material_regions.json \
    --point write_0_to_1 --out-dir out/sram_overview_write
```

Use a fresh output directory for each configuration. `--lateral-bc insulating`
selects the former sidewall treatment. The generic `BoundaryConditions` object
still defaults to nonperiodic sides; the two case scripts opt into periodicity.
Use `--mesh-method legacy` in a separate output directory for an explicit
bounding-box comparison. `--refine` multiplies the target mesh lengths; smaller
values request a finer newly generated mesh.

`--mesh-manifest` requires a sealed notebook manifest: the saved mesh file,
source GDS and instance correspondence are fingerprint-checked before use.
Current electrical powers are assigned on every run. This option preserves the
saved mesh's geometry and resolution; it does not remesh it with new settings.

The compact case prints power, source-volume checks, source/sink balance and
peak temperature. New notebook-mesh generation writes
`sram_sp_cell_material_regions.msh`, the matching `.json` manifest and mesh
statistics; the adapter saves `mesh_import_report.json`. The compact driver
writes `simulation_summary.json`, temperature slices and, unless disabled,
a geometry SVG. The legacy path retains `gds_volume.msh`.
Updated SRAM cases also save `source_power_audit.json`, recording electrical,
assigned and integrated power for each instance. The transient case additionally
saves temperature fields,
coordinates/connectivity and time-history metadata. Repeated runs can replace
files in their selected output directories.

## 8. What has been verified, and what remains uncertain

The earlier [periodic verification](PERIODIC_BOUNDARIES.md) records **34 passing
tests** covering numerical/specification checks, source accounting, analytical
conduction, GDS energy balance, periodic constraints and ngspice discovery.
The saved geometry comparisons separately verify the LI1 discrepancy above.
These are implementation checks, **not experimental validation of SRAM
temperatures**. The subsequent instance-mapping repair adds independent
connectivity and per-device power tests; its verification results are recorded
in [SRAM_INSTANCE_MAPPING.md](SRAM_INSTANCE_MAPPING.md). Historical cached
asymmetric temperature results must be regenerated with the corrected mapping.
The contact repair adds 11 focused tests, including exact preservation of the
real SRAM LICON polygons, and passes region-construction/material-overlap checks.
These checks alone do not certify a complete mesh or thermal solve; generated
meshes undergo their own validation before import.

The dominant qualifications are:

1. **Geometry:** the two migrated bitcell drivers preserve drawn polygons, but
   layer heights, contact profiles, wells and omitted process features remain
   simplified. Other SRAM drivers still have legacy bounding-box footprint
   errors. A faithful mask extrusion is not a verified fabricated cross-section.
2. **Power:** borrowed timing, estimated bitline capacitance and approximate
   local wire/contact resistance do not constitute full-array RC extraction.
   Electrical and thermal
   temperatures are not coupled. The repaired instance map is specific to the
   bundled cell; new layouts require independently verified correspondence.
3. **Cooling/domain:** `h`, substrate truncation and the assumed repeating
   workload strongly influence the result. A one-cell periodic case does not
   represent one active cell surrounded by idle neighbors.
4. **Materials/interfaces:** fixed conductivities, simplified layer thicknesses,
   omitted gate oxide and perfect interfaces need sensitivity checks or
   calibration for absolute-temperature prediction.
5. **Numerical resolution:** source conservation and linear-solver convergence
   do not replace spatial-mesh, timestep and domain-size convergence studies.
   Pulse sampling must preserve the intended energy.

For a more defensible SRAM prediction, choose an energy-consistent workload,
establish the actual array/repeat-unit and package boundary model, assess the
remaining process-geometry approximations, then perform convergence and
parameter-sensitivity checks. Polygon preservation and verified source identity
remove important implementation errors but do not resolve those assumptions.

**Bottom line:** Kelvin currently provides a useful layout-derived,
compact-model-informed thermal approximation. The core heat solver has
numerical checks, but the current SRAM geometry, electrical workload and
cooling assumptions do not yet justify a calibrated absolute-temperature claim.

For deeper findings, see [ACCURACY_REVIEW.md](ACCURACY_REVIEW.md). That review
predates the subsequent periodic-boundary work, ngspice installation,
solver-convergence guards, instance-map repair and notebook-mesh integration;
use this overview and the linked focused documents for those updated details.
